This small act of distribution is also an act of autonomy.

This, in fact, is going to be our first interactive activity I’ll share. I used GenAi to transform my power-point into an application that you can distribute to all your students or participants. Interactivity is often discussed as something that happens inside a classroom activity, but it can also happen in how we distribute materials, how we share resources, and how much control we give learners over the learning experience itself.

The presentation is no longer something I control, something you have to wait for it until the end of the session, or until I deliver it, or in Blackboard, where I control the speed of the delivery and all of its content, and you receive it as an audience. A feature of GenAI in teaching is that it can be used creatively to image new possibilities in the creation, co-creation and distribution of teaching materials, break the barrier between students and teacher, reduce the effect of students being ‘an audience’ and invite them to have access to the ‘teaching objects and activities’ differently.

Interactive teaching is not about AI

Before we discuss technology at all, the questions about interactivity in learning and teaching remain the same questions we have always asked ourselves as educators:

  • How will learning happen?
  • How will we know that learning has happened?
  • How will learning be inclusive to different needs, backgrounds and ways of engaging?
  • How will learning challenge, stimulate and transform students’ understandings of themselves and the world?

These are pedagogic questions.

They are questions of imagination, design, empathy, collaboration and reflexivity.

Whether we teach media, psychology art or design, interactive teaching begins with a sets of questions rather than a specific technology. Furthermore, her at Regent’s we have adopted a framework (RADAR) that address these questions and suggest an approach to how to design our teaching so it brings our teaching to be student-focused.

My use of GenAI for interactive learning and teaching is based upon the same principles of student centeredness and active learning. In fact, when I design an interactive session with GenAI I start by giving it both RADAR and our pedagogic principles before I describe the session I want to create.

Teaching has always relied on technologies

To bring pedagogy into the classroom we already rely on a great many technologies and frameworks: PowerPoint, Blackboard, Polling systems, quiz platforms, discussion boards, RADAR, images, maps, videsos, diagrams and simulations.

Digital teaching technologies help us enable perspectives. Think of any past or present technology, the projecting acetate, the poll, a visual cloud a metaverse room. They allow us to zoom in and out, to consider different angles, to rotate an object, to explore unseen places, compare viewpoints, visualise abstract concepts, to let the mind wonder aimlessly, sometimes to get lost, to make curiosity a habit.

The question is not whether we use teaching technologies. The question is what kind of digital technology and teaching space we are developing when we use GenAI?

Technology has always been part of teaching. In a teaching and learning context, AI is more than just a digital tool, it can be understood as a socio-technical learning environment that creates interactive, adaptative and conversational learnings spaces. It supports personalised learning, feedback generation, contente creaton, assessment support, knowledge exploration (interactive activities).

We say that Gen AI is a conversational learning environment (unlke Blackboard, Moodle) GenAI creates a dialogic space where learners can ask questions, receive immediate responses, explore ideas iteratively, engage in simulated discussions. It aligns with Vygotsky’s social constructivism where learning emerges through interaction and dialogue, so when we talk about ‘interactive’ we are talking about a digitally mediated learning space that enables learners to inteact with knowledge, scenarios, simulated expertise through natural language, and unlke static webpages, the space is dynamically generated in response to learner acttions.

A GenAI interaction is a syntethic learning environment in which educational content, interactions and learning pathways are generated in real time. The learning environment is not pre-built but it emerges, experimentally, through interaction between learner and AI.

What makes GenAI different?

Generative AI is not important because it is intelligent. It is important because it changes the relationship between educators and educational technology and the relationship between students and knowledge.

Remember adding pictures to a word document? You will all have experience of the tediousness of adding a picture and formatting text around a picture when creating a document, getting familiar with headings that misbehave. We struggle with Word because it is a software that is very limited, of which we have little power to change any of its configuration, you work with what you have been given and be thankful it has an autosave now. There is no autonomy from the software and how it was poorly designed.

The key point I want to make is that AI is not a software.

The real difference with using GenAI is not that it saves time either (will explore this later). My argument here is that with AI what changes is that you become, for the first time, autonomus of all the previous softwares, word, excel, powerpoint, the microsoft word, the world of obscure code writing and the expensive individual license solutions for interactive cd-roms.

With AI you can design something for you that is better designed because it responds to your needs and the problems you want tor resolve with them. You are not dependent on OpenAI or Anthropic or the companies behind it (even those with Open Access). It is an expensive autonomy.

However, with Gen Ai many of us can create the things we previously needed software permissions, specialist platforms, subscriptions or technical expertise to build: a simulation, a game, an interactive reading, a research application, a scavenger hunt, an interactive visualisation… we talk to GenAI about what we need, delegate the obscurity of coding and other dependencies to it, and produce new solutions for learning.

When I talk about GenAI interactive teaching I am talking of an interactive cognitive partner, a tool, thinking partner, learning companion, scaffold for learning. Rather than simply delivering content, GenAI participates in the learning process and can be used to create activities that augments the learning process.

GenAI is not just digitalizing existing educational practices, but reshaping the learning environment itself. The work we’ll try today is an adventure into being curious and reimagining our learning environment.

Some considerations – and some myths

Misconception one

AI saves time

Not really, what you gain with using AI is increased autonomy to design better teaching.

There is a learning curve. There are frustrations. There are emotional reactions to new technologies. There is experimentation, failure, revision and redesign.

However, for the first time, if you invest time on it (it won’t save you from some investment time) you get to create things for which you needed software permissions, company access, editing constrains, or had to hire people, to make you a bot, or to create a search engine, software for making a quiz in the class, subscription to wooclap.

A focus on time reduces the conversation to productivity. However, productivity is not the most interesting thing happening when we work with AI.

Misconception two

AI makes your thinking worse

This argument has accompanied almost every major technological change, there has always been a discourse on ‘loss’ (loss of humanity, of society, morality, ability to think, ability to be an independent agent) and a romanticization of the past. Calculators were said to do the same to the brain, so did computers, so did the internet to libraries and so on.

Anthropologically speaking, I find many of these claims difficult to separate from wider anxieties about technological change. I don’t believe that the use of GenAi makes your thinking ‘less’ than what you were doing before the use of GenAI. It does change how we think, but so did calculators changed how we calculate without them, the brain finds new connections.

People are worried about ‘cognitive offloading’ delegating thinking tasks to AI. people stop doing mental work themselves because a tool does it for them. You loose a skill you don’t practice? Or is this about authorship (ride a bike or ride it like a professional?). Cognitive debt, but also research proves positive outcomes on learning achievement, inclusivity, learning motivation.

The educational challenge is preventing students from outsourcing judgment, evaluation, and synthesis while still benefiting from AI-supported learning.

Misconception three

Prompting is Key

Prompting is often heralded as the key to succesful use of AI, however there is a bit of a misconception around this and prompting is treated as a kind of specialised knowledge and a kind of magic formula.

Prompting does not make AI more intelligent. It helps it use its existing capabilities more effectively.

People often search for the perfect prompt. There is not one.

Prompting is iterative. Prompting is contextual. Prompting depends on goals. The most useful prompts are rarely the cleverest. They are usually the clearest.

In many ways prompting resembles educational design itself. It is the process of creating a conversation that helps someone think, explore, critique and create.

If you are not sure where to start with GenAI and creating interactive activities, start with a conversation with AI.

Activity 4: Take your AI Interactive activities to Blackboard

Copy and page your AI generated artifacts (html) from Claude to Blackboard in less than 30 seconds.

We have created today various activities, but how do you use a gen ai created interactive activity and put it in Blackboard (for phone or computer)?

Here below is how you get an ai generated artifact from Claude to Blackboard:

  1. Converse with Claude on what you need for teaching. Ask Claude to create any activity as a HTML artifact or HTML page.
  2. Once you are happy with the activity, it will become visible in the right hand side canvas. Click on the little eye and select ‘copy’, at the top of the canva.
  3. Open Blackboard and select ‘create new page’.
  4. Inside the page select ‘html’, then paste the code you copied from Claude.
  5. Save and watch.
